# LoadLink design direction

The product stays simple enough for a driver or owner-operator to use with one hand and no training. Visual polish must clarify state; it must not add navigation, choices, or decoration that competes with the primary action.

## Component language

- Use generous white space, soft off-white surfaces, quiet borders and shallow shadows. Cards should feel like physical shipping labels, not generic dashboard tiles.
- Use small logistics-specific illustrations for route, pickup, cargo and vehicle state. Prefer LoadLink-owned SVG marks over a generic icon inside a colored square when the object carries meaning.
- Pair numbers with short labels: price, distance, rate and capacity remain the visual hierarchy.
- Express route progress with a thin solid/dotted line and distinct origin/destination shapes. Do not depend on color alone.
- Keep labels conversational and concrete: “Fits your van”, “Volume unknown”, “Along the way”.
- On mobile, preserve the three destinations Find · Trips · Account, 44 px targets and one primary action per screen.
- Desktop may use more horizontal space, but must not introduce a permanent sidebar or additional product areas.

## Reference interpretation

The supplied shipping references inform card proportions, subdued isometric freight art, progress lines and compact labels. LoadLink retains its blue accent and existing product flow. We do not copy their green palette, page structure, rewards, search chrome or ornamental features.
